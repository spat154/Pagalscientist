## How each competitor's engine actually works

### The Australia Today — the one to respect
The fastest-growing and most dangerous competitor. Its engine is **breaking news
plus accountability/investigative journalism** aimed at the diaspora:
- Their single biggest traffic page is an investigation into a migration agent
  being deregistered ("Bajwa", 9,900/mo search, ~374 visits/mo from it alone).
- They chase deportations, crime (fatal crashes, the Bondi gunman, shootings),
  political scandal (the Preet Singh saga, ASIO, NDIS fraud bans), and
  India–Australia geopolitics (Indus Waters Treaty, "India to build 1 million
  homes in Australia").
- A June 2026 spike to ~38,000 visits shows they ride viral national news hard.

**Takeaway:** they win on speed, controversy and original reporting. Do **not**
try to out-scandal them — that needs a newsroom and carries legal/defamation
risk. Beat them on the evergreen and service content they neglect.

### Indian Link — the evergreen incumbent (full analysis in earlier brief)
Highest authority and traffic. Engine = evergreen **temples, Bollywood/film
reviews, diaspora identity essays, festival explainers, events directory**. Ranks
on reputation, not SEO discipline — sits at position 15–20 on its biggest-volume
temple terms. Beatable on quality and structure.

### The Indian Sun — the hyperlocal play
High authority (DA 41), modest traffic. Engine = **hyperlocal Melbourne
community news**, especially the Indian-heavy western suburbs (Wyndham, Point
Cook, Manor Lakes, Truganina), plus local business openings (Coles, Cadbury,
Saravanaa Bhavan), temples and India–Australia relations. Date-stamped news URLs,
high publishing cadence, lots of thin posts ranking low.

**Takeaway:** they own western-Melbourne hyperlocal. Either avoid that lane or
beat it with better-structured suburb guides.

### South Asia Times — the film-review machine
Engine = **Bollywood/Tamil/OTT film and Netflix reviews** (Single Papa, Court,
Sector 36, Sholay, Maharaj, dozens more) plus **community/temple** coverage
(ranks #2 for "akshardham melbourne", strong on BAPS Melbourne) and occasional
breaking community news (immigration raid at a Carrum Downs temple). Clean
category URL structure (`/entertainment-sports/cinema/...`). Their review engine
pulls steady, repeatable traffic on a shoestring.

### Indusage — fading, but instructive
Tiny traffic now, poor permalink structure (`?p=12345`). Worth noting its
longest-lived winners are **utility/service pages** — a "where to buy an Opal
card" page still pulls search traffic years later, and "ZEE5 subscription price
in Australia" ranks well. Service content compounds; nobody is doing it properly.

## The patterns that win across every competitor

1. **Hindu temples are the universal evergreen goldmine.** Every single outlet
   ranks for temple queries (BAPS/Akshardham, Swaminarayan, Sri Durga, Ganesha,
   Murugan), and the big-volume terms (murugan temple Sydney 27,100/mo; shiv
   mandir Sydney 14,800) are held weakly by all of them. This is the clearest
   shared opportunity.
2. **Film/OTT reviews** = cheap, repeatable, release-driven traffic (South Asia
   Times, Indusage, Indian Link all rely on it; you barely do it).
3. **Breaking community news and scandal** = traffic spikes and authority, but
   resource-heavy and risky (The Australia Today, The Indian Sun).
4. **Hyperlocal suburb news** in Indian-dense areas = loyal, low-competition
   (The Indian Sun).
5. **Concerts, garba and artist tours** = seasonal surges — and this is already
   *your* strength (Geeta Rabari, Aishwarya Majmudar, Rahat Fateh Ali Khan).
6. **Service/utility evergreen** (transport, visa/passport, "flying home",
   streaming, settling-in) = high-volume, compounding, and **almost nobody owns
   it properly.** This is the biggest white space in the category.
7. **India–Australia macro stories** (trade, "1 million homes", migration) rank
   across multiple outlets — diaspora readers want the "what it means for us"
   angle.

## Where Made in India should compete (and where not)

**Lean in (compounding, winnable, on-brand):**
- **Definitive city temple hubs** — beat everyone on the biggest-volume temple
  terms with better structure and accuracy.
- **Practical service journalism** — the category's biggest white space: visa
  and passport how-tos, sending money home, transport, "flying home" logistics,
  streaming/ZEE5, settling-in guides. Pure Pillar 1.
- **Events & garba** — defend and extend your existing niche with per-city,
  per-festival hubs.
- **Same-day film/OTT reviews** — cheap traffic South Asia Times proves works.
- **Food guides** — you already rank (food trucks, desserts, restaurants); make
  them definitive.
- **Festival explainers** — seasonal, evergreen, perfectly on-brand.

**Be careful / avoid:**
- **Out-scandalling The Australia Today** — investigative accountability needs a
  newsroom and carries defamation risk. Cover community news factually, but don't
  build the strategy on chasing scandal.
- **Generic "Australian news" terms** — The Australia Today already burns effort
  ranking page-3 for these; they don't serve your pillars.

**Your unfair advantage:** you have real GA/GSC data and (once the engine is
live) disciplined, sourced, RankMath-clean, Australian-English content. The whole
category is winning on reputation and luck, not craft. Craft is how a DA-22 site
climbs.
